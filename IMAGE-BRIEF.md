# Replacement imagery brief

What to request so the truck photography stops contradicting the website.

## The problem

Four of the seven truck images show **613-371-3785** on the wrap. The site now
publishes **1-888-912-5152**. A visitor reads one number in the header and a
different one on the truck beside it.

I retouched the one image where it was achievable — the large tailgate shot,
where the panel is flat, dark and evenly lit, and the digits sit horizontal. It
holds at the sizes the site uses.

I attempted two more and reverted both. They came out visibly worse than the
originals: on the bed close-up the digits run at a steep angle my replacement
could not match and the old "3785" ghosted through; on the two-technician shot
the number sits on a green gradient panel and the fill sampled grey. Shipping
those would have looked like bad retouching, which reads worse than a wrong
number.

I did not attempt the logo swap. Mapping the real logo — red target, tick, two
lines of type — onto angled panels with matching perspective and lighting, across
seven images, is not something I can deliver to a standard you would want on a
homepage.

## What to request instead

Regenerate the four affected images, or better, photograph the real truck. Ask
for exactly this:

- **Phone on the wrap: `1-888-912-5152`.** Nothing else.
- **Website: `eco-ticksolutions.ca`.**
- **One logo, used consistently.** The current set mixes two marks — a red
  circle-slash tick in some, a green leaf in others. Neither matches the real
  logo in `public/images/eco-tick/brand/`. Pick one and use it on every panel.
- **Keep the composition.** The existing framing works: truck on a residential
  driveway, tank and reel visible, technician mid-application.
- **1800px wide minimum**, landscape 3:2.

## Better still

The strongest image on the site is the small photograph of Edward on the tractor
— because it is unmistakably real. Everything else reads as generated on close
inspection. A morning with a camera and the actual truck would outperform every
image in this folder, and would cost nothing but the morning.

Shots worth getting: the rig from behind with the tank and reel in frame, a
technician running hose along a treeline, the truck on a customer driveway, and
one clean detail of the pump and gauge.
